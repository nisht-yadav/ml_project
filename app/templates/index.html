What am I looking at?

Grad-CAM highlights the pixels that most influenced this specific model's decision. It explains the CNN, not the image: if the model is wrong, Grad-CAM will still point at whatever it learned to look for.

ELA (Error Level Analysis) re-saves the image as JPEG at a fixed quality and shows where the result differs most from the original. Regions that were compressed at a different history (spliced in from another photo, or edited and re-saved) tend to light up brighter than their surroundings. Plain image processing, no model involved.

DCT (frequency analysis) splits the image into blocks and measures how much high-frequency detail each block contains. Blocks with unusually high or low energy relative to their neighbors can indicate re-compression or a spliced-in region. Plain image processing, no model involved.

ELA and DCT are shown alongside Grad-CAM because they're independent of the CNN: they can flag a suspicious region even when the model gets the label wrong.
